1. Project Objective & Multi-Sensory Architecture

The goal of this assignment is to develop interactive spatial interaction mechanics by building a face-tracked social AR experience (CO3) and engineering an architectural framework for a multi-sensory AR gaming prototype utilizing 3D spatial audio (HRTF) and tactile haptic feedback (CO4).

Human perception in Augmented Reality depends critically on multi-modal sensory binding: visual anchoring creates presence, Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTF) provide natural spherical localization to guide player attention toward off-screen targets, and tactile vibration triggers deliver physical confirmation for touch and gaze interactions.

2. Part A — Interactive Face Tracking & Social AR Experience (CO3)

2.1 Facial Landmark Anchoring Mechanics

The social AR experience tracks 468 3D facial landmarks in real-time, executing responsive gesture recognition and procedural asset anchoring across three distinct anatomical regions:

  • Forehead Anchor (3D Holographic Crown): Anchored to landmark index 10 (forehead center) with an offset vector (0, +0.08m, 0). Orientation angles (Pitch, Yaw, Roll) are calculated to rotate the 3D crown synchronously with user head movements.
  • Eye Landmark Visor HUD & Blink Trigger: Anchored across landmarks 33, 263, and 168. Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) continuously monitors eyelid distance:
    EAR = (||p159 - p145|| + ||p386 - p374||) / (2 * ||p263 - p33||)
    When EAR falls below 0.022, an eye blink event triggers a reticle color transition from neon cyan to glowing plasma magenta.
  • Mouth Openness Ratio & Plasma Energy Beam: Computed via Euclidean distance between upper inner lip (13) and lower inner lip (14). When openness exceeds 35% of face height, a procedural plasma beam discharges with animated particle shockwaves, accompanied by spatial audio and haptic rumble.

2.2 Viewport Constraints & Social Engagement Design

  • Viewport Bounds in 9:16 Displays: Mobile screens restrict horizontal FoV. To prevent jarring pops when a user tilts their head out of view, linear interpolation (lerp) damping is applied to prevent asset clipping near boundaries.
  • Micro-Interactions & Virality: The combination of natural facial gestures (mouth opening, winking, head nodding) turns the user's face into an intuitive controller, fostering high-engagement social sharing.

3. Part B — Spatial Audio & Haptics Architectural Design (CO4)

3.1 HRTF vs. Standard Stereo Panning Comparison

Standard stereo panning relies solely on amplitude scaling (Interaural Level Difference) along a 1D horizontal axis:

Gain_Left = cos( (π * (θ + 90°)) / 360° ),    Gain_Right = sin( (π * (θ + 90°)) / 360° )

This model suffers from severe front-back ambiguity ("cone of confusion") and cannot render vertical elevation. It results in sounds feeling "inside the player's skull" (lateralization) rather than located in physical space.

In contrast, Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTF) simulate complete 3D binaural acoustic diffraction around the listener’s head, torso, and external ear pinna:

y_Left(t) = x(t) * h_Left(θ, φ, d, t),     y_Right(t) = x(t) * h_Right(θ, φ, d, t)

Spatial Localization 1D horizontal axis (left / right only). True 3D spherical space (Azimuth, Elevation, Distance).
Interaural Time Difference (ITD) Absent (0 ms phase shift). Accurately synthesized (Δt up to 0.7 ms).
Interaural Level Difference (ILD) Basic linear/sinusoidal gain balance. Frequency-dependent acoustic head-shadow filtering.
Spectral Pinna Cues None (flat frequency response). Directional spectral notches (4–10 kHz) resolve front/back.

3.2 Unity Spatial Audio Off-Screen Guidance & Attenuation Model

In mobile AR, off-screen targets must be acoustically beaconed to guide player gaze back into the camera viewport. The system implements an inverse-distance attenuation curve:

Gain(d) = d_min / (d_min + α * (d - d_min))    [where d_min = 1.0m, d_max = 25.0m, α = 1.2]

Additionally, an Acoustic Head-Shadow Low-Pass Filter attenuates high frequencies when the target lies behind the player (|θ| > 90°):

f_cutoff(θ) = Lerp(22000 Hz, 2500 Hz, (|θ| / 180°)^1.5)

As the player rotates their head toward the target, the cutoff frequency opens up and beacon ping frequency increases, providing immediate intuitive biofeedback.
